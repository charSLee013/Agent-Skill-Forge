# Course Composition

Compose the course from the learner's target capability and the subject's internal structure. Read `editorial-direction.md` before assigning prose responsibilities and `visual-motion-direction.md` before assigning representations.

## Domain Capability Model

Infer the expert work before dividing the course into pages:

- tasks and decisions;
- evidence, standards, tools, and observations used in those decisions;
- prerequisite knowledge and embodied skills;
- novice errors and expert failure diagnoses;
- the artifact that demonstrates expert judgment.

Use domain-native evidence. A research course may culminate in peer review, a cooking course in execution and sensory diagnosis, and a codebase course in architecture review, failure reproduction, repair, and verification.

## Learner Path

Map the learner's current abilities against the graduation task. Add a diagnostic entry, prerequisite bridge, or narrower milestone wherever the next mechanism otherwise requires guessing.

Build a progression through:

```text
orient -> bridge -> explain -> reconstruct -> compare -> diagnose -> practice -> transfer -> capstone
```

The sequence may fold or repeat stages. Keep the causal dependency visible.

## Course Architecture

Write one memorable main trunk. Map prerequisite and concept dependencies. Give each page one primary learning responsibility and a clear handoff to the next capability.

Vary page composition by its role: orientation, foundation, mechanism, evidence, practice, transfer, synthesis, reference, deep dive, or capstone. Do not repeat a fixed set of headings across page types.

For Ultra, surround the Core Path with:

- **Prerequisite Bridge**: mathematics, vocabulary, tools, procedures, and intuition;
- **Deep Dives**: derivations, implementation, history, edge cases, and advanced mechanisms;
- **Reference Atlas**: terminology, formulas, standards, parameters, and comparisons;
- **Evidence Map**: claims, sources, observations, derivations, and scope;
- **Expert Branches**: disputes, alternatives, open questions, and further inquiry.

## Structured Subjects

For repositories, systems, protocols, papers, workflows, tools, and physical practices, map:

- purpose, outcomes, interfaces, and inputs;
- representations, vocabulary, processes, and state changes;
- constraints, environment, evidence, and verification;
- failure modes, repairs, expert comparisons, and open questions.

Use `../templates/structured-object-map.md` as the planning surface.

## Mechanisms and Representations

For each meaningful mechanism, identify the learner's hardest relationship. Explain the object, inputs, variables, initial state, one step, whole process, constraints, and failure modes.

Choose prose for causality, formulas for precise relationships, tables for comparison, traces for temporal change, diagrams for structure, worked examples for reconstruction, and direct manipulation for consequential state changes. Use `../templates/mechanism-visual-spec.md` when a representation carries a learning dependency.

## Practice and Feedback

Move practice from recall toward expert judgment:

1. retrieve the main idea;
2. reconstruct a mechanism or procedure;
3. apply it to a worked case;
4. compare alternatives;
5. diagnose a failure;
6. adapt the idea to a new context;
7. produce a domain-native artifact.

Provide graduated feedback through hints, diagnostic rules, partial solutions, worked expert examples, rubrics, and self-review prompts.

## Capstone

Define one authentic expert task. Supply inputs, constraints, expected artifacts, evaluation dimensions, common failure patterns, staged checkpoints, and an expert-quality example or review.

## Page Responsibility

Give each page:

- a place in the main trunk and one primary learning responsibility;
- prerequisite links and assigned content objects;
- an editorial arc and representation purpose;
- evidence, scope, practice, or learner action;
- ordinary previous, map, and next navigation;
- a handoff to the next capability.

Complete the architecture when every content object, source role, learner action, and transition has one owner.
